## Abstract / Summary
Chris Lehane argues that California, New York, and Illinois have advanced converging frontier safety legislation—safety frameworks with risk assessments and disclosure, serious-incident reporting, and independent audits—creating reverse federalism toward a de facto national standard. The post urges avoiding patchwork mission creep while federal cyber-testing standards and CAISI capacity take shape, and supports bipartisan congressional work that can underpin a US-led international AI standards forum. Core thesis: democratic governments, not labs alone, should set critical frontier-safety decisions so tools reach trusted defenders without chaotic state-by-state fragmentation.
